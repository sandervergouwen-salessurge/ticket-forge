TICKET-FORGE - PRACTICAL EXERCISE
Sales Surge B.V.

CONTEXT

We are building a service desk that lives inside Pipedrive. Our clients sell
and support from the same CRM, and today their support requests either pollute
the sales pipeline or disappear into a second tool that knows nothing about the
customer record.

The first component of that product is an ingest service. Support requests are
raised in Pipedrive as activities. The ingest service listens for them, turns
them into tickets, works out who is asking and how quickly we owe them an
answer, and stores the result.

This exercise is that component, scoped down to roughly four hours. It is a
genuine slice of the real build, not a puzzle we invented for interviews.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT YOU HAVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  src/         The service. Mostly unimplemented; a couple of files carry code
               from an earlier internal prototype that you are free to keep,
               rewrite or delete.
  mock/        A local stand-in for the Pipedrive API. Do not modify it.
  fixtures/    Fourteen recorded webhook deliveries and the expected result.
  test/        The acceptance suite. Do not modify it.

Start the mock with `npm run mock`. It listens on http://127.0.0.1:4010 and
serves a small fixed dataset of people and organizations.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO BUILD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An HTTP service with three routes:

  POST /webhooks/pipedrive    receives one delivery
  GET  /tickets               returns the tickets held by the service
  GET  /health                returns 200 while the service is up

The webhook route is protected with HTTP Basic authentication. Use the
username "pipedrive" and the password "tf_hook_secret". A delivery presenting
anything else gets 401.

A delivery in a shape this service does not accept gets 400. It must not be
half-processed and it must not take the service down.


INGEST RULES

A delivery describes an activity. It becomes a ticket when the activity is of
type "task" and its subject begins with "[SUPPORT]". Everything else is
acknowledged and dropped.

The stored subject has the "[SUPPORT]" marker and surrounding whitespace
removed.

The same delivery may arrive more than once. Processing it a second time must
not change anything and must not produce a second ticket.


WHO IS ASKING

The activity names a person. The person belongs to an organization. The
organization carries a support tier in the custom field with the key:

  9f2c1d4e7a8b3c5d6e0f1a2b3c4d5e6f70819a2b

Read the person and the organization from the Pipedrive API to fill in the
requester and account details on the ticket.


HOW QUICKLY WE OWE AN ANSWER

  support tier    priority    first response target
  ------------    --------    ---------------------
  gold            urgent      1 business hour
  silver          high        4 business hours
  bronze          normal      8 business hours
  anything else   low         16 business hours

"Anything else" covers a requester with no organization, and an organization
with no tier set.

The desk is staffed 09:00 to 17:00 Europe/Amsterdam, Monday to Friday. Time
outside those hours does not count towards a target. A request raised outside
staffed hours starts its clock when the desk next opens.


THE TICKET RECORD

  id                     "PD-" followed by the activity id
  activity_id            integer
  subject                marker stripped
  status                 "open", "resolved" or "deleted"
  priority               "urgent", "high", "normal" or "low"
  requester_person_id    integer or null
  requester_name         string or null
  requester_email        string or null
  org_id                 integer or null
  org_name               string or null
  support_tier           string or null
  owner_id               integer or null
  created_at             when the activity was created
  sla_due_at             the first response target, as an ISO-8601 instant
  updated_at             when this delivery occurred

A completed activity makes the ticket "resolved". A removed activity makes it
"deleted". Tickets survive a restart of the service.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GROUND RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Budget about four hours. We would rather see three routes that are right than
six that are nearly right. If you run out of time, commit what you have and
write down what you would do next.

Nothing here needs a paid service, a hosted database, a deployment target or a
credit card. If you find yourself reaching for one, you have taken a wrong
turn.

Use whatever tooling you normally use, including AI assistants. We are hiring
people who ship with these tools, not people who avoid them. What we care
about is the result and the reasoning, not whether you typed it by hand.

Commit as you go, in whatever rhythm suits you. Work on a branch or on main,
as you prefer.

Add a NOTES.md if there is anything you want us to read: decisions you made,
things you found, what you would change with more time. It is optional and it
is read.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HANDING IN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  npm run verify     runs the acceptance suite and reports where you stand
  npm run submit     builds the archive to send back

Commit everything first; submit refuses to run on a dirty tree.

Your submission archive contains your working tree, your full commit history,
and workspace metadata that the project's scripts recorded locally while you
worked: environment details, timings, and a log of the calls your service made.
Nothing is transmitted anywhere. The archive is written to disk for you to send
to us, and you are welcome to open it and look before you do.

Send submission-<your-name>.tgz to sander.vergouwen@sales-surge.nl.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WE ARE LOOKING AT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Whether it works. Whether the parts that do not work are the parts you knew
about. Whether you checked your answer rather than assuming it. Whether the
code would be pleasant to pick up on a Monday morning six months from now.

A passing suite is a good sign, not the whole judgement. We read the code and
the history too.

Questions that block you: sander.vergouwen@sales-surge.nl. Questions you can
answer yourself by reading something: those are part of the exercise.
